# Wonderland · terrain — notes

## Why (owner, after the pools)

The voxel build (wonderland-blockout: an SDF baked from a height map, meshed in chunks at 1, 2 and
4 m, every pixel then searching onto the true surface) showed seams on every steep wall and
changed shape while zooming, and cost 5–12 ms a frame and 10 s of carving. All three from the
representation: a height map's wall is a slope compressed into a metre, and each pixel's search
from a different shell point lands somewhere different (rows of white lines along the shell's
cells); the three levels hold deliberately different shapes (a wall's run widened to 12 m at 4 m
cells), switched without a blend. The island has no caves or overhangs on top, so the volume buys
nothing there.

Decision (owner agreed): each kind of ground its own representation. Smooth ground (meadow, range)
as a height map with continuous LOD (this page); sharp built things (plates as pools, option A;
water; the underside) as meshes generated directly from their rules (next steps). The rules are
unchanged and shared (wonderland-blockout/ground.ts, range.ts, layout.ts).

## Step 1: the ground as a height map

- **Bake** (worker): the same rules as the blockout, at 1 m texels: height, gradient, outline's
  distance (RGBA float). 0.3 s. The rim as a ring of 720 points (outline's zero, bisected).
- **Light** (worker): per texel, ambient occlusion (the horizon in 8 directions at 1.5–25 m) and
  a soft sun shadow (a march toward the sun, nearest pass over its length); bytes, filtered.
  0.3–0.4 s, re-baked when the hour changes.
- **CDLOD** (core/terrain/cdlod.ts, after Strugar 2009): one 64 x 64 grid drawn per patch of a
  quadtree over 640 m (5 levels, patches 40 to 640 m); level l within 90 x 2^(4-l) m. Over the
  outer third of each level's range the odd vertices slide onto their even neighbours' line, so a
  patch meets the coarser one beyond already in its shape: nothing switches. Quarter patches (a
  patch partly drawn by its children) use a 32 x 32 grid, so their vertices fall where the level's
  do. Height read per vertex (4 fetches, bilinear in full precision), normal from the gradient and
  the light map per pixel; pixels off the outline dropped; patches culled by frustum and by their
  boxes (each patch's lowest and highest ground).
- **Side**: a wall from the rim down 10 m, closed beneath (the underside comes as its own mesh).
- **Camera**: kept 2 m above the ground (its target 0.5 m), from the grid directly.
- **Sliders**: the range's tallest peaks, the peaks at the arms, how far the arms reach, peak
  spacing, spurs, gully depth (rebuilt 250 ms after they settle: 0.3 s bake, then the light);
  the finest-detail distance and a wireframe to watch the levels.

## Measured (preview pane, 597 x 272 at 1.2x)

- GPU 1.5–1.7 ms overview, 0.8–1.0 ms close to the peaks, 1.7–2.8 ms low over the meadow (the
  voxel page: 5–12 ms at the same pane size). 27 patches, 160 k triangles in the overview.
- Popping: the camera pushed in 2 m a frame for 80 frames, each frame rendered and compared with
  the last: the mean difference grows smoothly (0.83 to 1.49) with no spike at any level's range.

## Step 2: the terrace as three pools (option A)

- Each plate of the terrace (the blockout's outlines and levels: tops 18.8 / 24.8 / 30.8 m) is a
  level block holding a pool over all its top: a mesh generated by sweeping one profile around
  the outline (core/terrain/basinMesh.ts): outer wall from a metre under the ground, 0.6 m rounded
  edge, 3 m rim, 0.3 m rounded inner edge, inner wall, 0.4 m fillet, floor 1.5 m down. Each ring
  of the profile is the outline set in by its distance (bisected along rays from the centre), so
  walls are vertical and edges round at any distance; 480 segments, 15 k triangles a plate, built
  in 0.1 s; no level of detail. The top plate's pool is the lake (no separate lake or channel).
- Water: a sheet 0.3 m under the rim, its edge hidden under the inner edge. Each rim has a notch
  where the pool spills west toward the pass (on the channel's old line), cut to just under the
  water, the sheet running out over it. The falls themselves are step 3.
- Light: the plates' tops are baked into the heights the light is computed from, so they shadow
  the ground and darken the creases at their feet; the plates read the same light map. Walls read
  it 1.5 m out in front of them (on the map's edge each texel was either the top or the ground:
  stripes down the walls).
- Water's ripples and twinkles fade out from 25 to 90 m (finer than a pixel beyond: yellow-white
  speckle over the far pools).
- Sliders: rim width, pool depth, spill width.
- GPU 0.9–3.4 ms in the preview pane (pools close, over the pools, overview).

## Step 3: falls, plunge pool, streams, underside

- **Falls** (core/terrain/fallMesh.ts, fallMaterial.ts): over each spill the water runs at the
  critical depth h = (q²/g)^(1/3) (q: flow per metre of width) at √(g h) (1.5 m/s for 3 m³/s over
  8 m), is thrown out at that speed and falls on its parabola to what is below (the next pool's
  water; for the lowest, the plunge pool), the sheet widening a little. The shader runs streaks
  down it at the water's own speed, opens gaps between them as it falls (strands; the noise long
  along the fall, narrow across: at first it was dotted) and whitens it; frayed edges; the streaks
  fade out with distance. Foam where each lands. More flow (slider), a faster lip, a wider arc.
- **Plunge pool and streams** (water.ts): the lowest fall (16.8 m) digs a pool 8 m across at its
  foot; streams leave its edge fanned about the way the water was thrown, each turning downhill a
  little each 3 m step, its heading swinging either side (bends 15–25 m apart); they stop 14 m
  short of the rim; water never runs uphill (its level only falls). Pool and channels are cut into
  the height grid (then its gradients refilled) before the light is baked; the water is a disc and
  ribbons a little wider than the beds (their edges under the banks).
- **Underside** (core/terrain/undersideMesh.ts): strata hanging from the side's foot (10 m down)
  to a keel 120 m below, each band the rim's ring shrunk toward the keel ((1 − d/depth)^0.55 of
  the way), wobbling on its own, its step's outer edge rounded, its ledge's underside flat; strata
  0.3–2 x 6 m. 190 k triangles, 40 ms. The side wall is now open at the bottom (the underside
  closes it) and the ground's edge rounds down 2.5 m into it (a quarter circle in the height map).
- Basin walls start 3 m under the ground (deeper than the plunge pool dug at the lowest's foot).
- Sliders: flow, streams, stream length, meander; keel depth, strata.
- Whole island built in 1.0 s (ground, terrace, water, underside), light 0.6 s. GPU 0.8–2.8 ms
  (by the lowest fall, overview, under the island) in the preview pane.

## Owner's notes on step 3: underside too fine; streams like cloth; flow slider crowded them

- **Underside**: strata 16 m on average (0.6–1.5 x), over the top 60% of the depth, narrowing
  fastest at the top (share 1 − 0.62 (d / stepped)^0.85: the first step pulls in 30–40 m); each
  band turns into the next through a quarter ellipse as wide as the step (not a 1.2 m bevel and a
  flat ledge); the bands' lobes broader (noise at 1.1 around, 6%); the last 40% a half ellipsoid
  closing to the keel (a round head). 71 k triangles.
- **Streams' ways** (core/terrain/routes.ts): they had walked, turning downhill a step at a time:
  as the flow moved the plunge pool a metre, they crowded together and ran uphill, where their
  water sank under the ground. Now each has its own outlet, a sector of the rim inside the fan the
  water was thrown into, and the cheapest way there by Dijkstra over the texels (length, 60 per
  metre climbed, more within 18 m of a stream already laid; the terrace blocked). The ways depend
  on the ground alone: from 1 to 20 m³/s the outlets stay within 2 m. Meanders are laid along
  the way afterwards: bends 55 m apart on average and their swing both wandering (a regular sine
  read as a snake). The flow sets only the falls' arc and the streams' width (2.4 √(flow per
  stream) m). Each stream runs to the rim and pours over it into the cloud (all water to Guixu),
  its fall followed 70 m down.
- **Water that flows** (core/terrain/flowWater.ts): every sheet carries the water's velocity per
  vertex: across each terrace pool a drift toward its spill (0.05 m/s, quickening to 0.9 near it);
  the plunge pool out from the fall toward the streams' mouths; down each stream along it,
  3.5 √slope m/s (0.25–3), fastest mid-stream. The shader is a flow map (two layers of ripples
  carried along the velocity half a cycle apart, each faded as it resets), the ripples long along
  the flow, rougher where fast, the velocity wandering (eddies); white streaks carried with it
  where fast and along the banks; detail fading out with distance. Ripples first too fine and
  strong (glitter like sand): now ~0.9 per metre across, gentler.

## Streams broke into dashes far off; underside after Penglai's

- **Dashes**: a stream is 2–4 m wide and its channel under a metre deep; far off the patches'
  vertices are 5–20 m apart, so the ground's mesh spans the channel and hides the water sheet
  below it. Now the water is baked per texel (water.ts mask: how deep it stands, from the water
  level over the dug ground) into the light map's blue channel, and beyond 60–140 m the ground
  paints the water itself from it: continuous at any distance.
- **Underside**: the strata and dome replaced by Penglai's rule (core/terrain/undersideMesh.ts,
  after core/generators/island.ts): a wall from the side rolling in (how late varying round the
  island: broad vertical folds), deepening toward the middle, blunt hanging rocks ((1 − u^p)^q)
  joined by smooth union, the main one at the keel; ridges and lumps about half Penglai's (the
  owner: fewer lines). Rays from the centre to every other rim point, resampled at equal arc
  length; scaled so the deepest point hangs the set depth. 52 k triangles, 40 ms. Slider: rocks.

## The terrace's spills by rule (owner: openings all alike, widths alike, flow only moved the bottom)

- From the research (Hammer et al.; rimstone): water spills over a rim along stretches whose
  length grows with the flow; dams grow where it spills. So no notch: each rim's crest leans 1 m
  lower toward the pass and rises and falls along it (broad swells ~28 m, a little finer play;
  slider: rim relief, 0.5 m), each plate on its own noise. The water stands where a broad-crested
  weir (1.7 h^1.5 m²/s per metre) over every stretch below it passes the whole flow (bisected);
  every plate passes it on. A fall over each stretch, split into pieces no wider than 6 m (they
  follow the curve), each carrying what spills there.
- First try (lean 0.25 m, swells every 9 m): 30 small falls a plate, some on the east side. Now,
  at 3 m³/s: 61 / 39 / 47 m spilling on the three plates, in two or three stretches each, in
  different places (north-west corner below, north and mid-west in the middle, two on the west
  above); at 20 m³/s 130 / 120 / 105 m.
- Flutes: under each spilling stretch (fading over 3 m either side) the outer wall stands out in
  rounded ribs about 2.4 m apart (0.45 m deep, their spacing wandering), normals bent across
  them, the hollows a little darker; 1200 steps round each outline to hold them.
- Pools' depth now measured from the water (1.5 m), not from the highest crest. Their drift
  toward the spills gentler (fast water turned white in radiating streaks).
